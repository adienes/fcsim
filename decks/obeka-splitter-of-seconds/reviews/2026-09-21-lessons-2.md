# Lessons from 2026-09-21_182210_pod-miirym-obeka-wilhelt-fire-lord-zuko_0042

- **commander_timing** Suspend Inspiring Refrain (or Ancestral Vision, Profane Tutor or Suspended Sentence) on turn 2 or 3, then cast Obeka on turn 4 and attack with her on turn 5. Every extra upkeep from her hit removes a time counter, so the suspended card that was sitting in exile is her first payoff. _Evidence: Game 0042. Round 3: it suspended Inspiring Refrain. Round 4: it cast Obeka. Round 5: Obeka hit Fire Lord Zuko for 2, and the two extra upkeeps took Refrain from 2 counters to 0. Refrain was cast for free and drew Replicating Ring and Court of Locthwain._
- **sequencing** On the turn Obeka will connect, put every upkeep trigger in place before combat: cast the Court, suspend Ancestral Vision and Suspended Sentence in main phase 1. Her extra upkeeps happen that same turn, right after combat, so anything cast in main phase 2 misses them. _Evidence: Game 0042. Round 7: it suspended Ancestral Vision (4 counters) and Suspended Sentence (3 counters) before attacking. Four extra upkeeps then fired Sentence, which killed Undead Warchief and cost Wilhelt 3 life, and cast Vision to draw 3. Round 8: it cast Sol Ring and Court of Ire in main phase 1 and became monarch, so Court of Ire dealt 7 in the first extra upkeep and finished Wilhelt._
- **combat** Send Obeka at whichever opponent has no untapped creatures, or only one, since her menace means a single creature can't block her. A 2-damage hit is worth two whole extra upkeeps. Pick the target by blockers, not by life total. _Evidence: Game 0042. Round 5: Zuko had 0 untapped blockers, and Obeka hit him for 2. Round 6: Wilhelt had 0, and she hit for 2. Round 7: Wilhelt's only untapped creature was a decayed Zombie, which can't block, and she hit again. She connected every time she attacked, and every hit was worth a pile of triggers._
- **how_a_card_works** Twilight Prophet makes each opponent lose X life, but you gain only X once, not X for each opponent. Count your life that way whenever it fires in several extra upkeeps. _Evidence: Game 0042, round 7: three extra-upkeep Prophet reveals of mana value 2 gained the pilot 4 life each time instead of 2. The auditor had it reset its life from 44 to 38._

## How it was piloted, game by game

- `2026-09-21_182210_pod-miirym-obeka-wilhelt-fire-lord-zuko_0042` **Keep:** It took one mulligan and kept seven cards: 4 lands (Swamp, Smoldering Marsh, Island, Shivan Reef), Arcane Signet, Creeping Bloodsucker and Thassa. That is 5 mana sources, two plays costing two, and an upkeep engine piece. By the primer's rules this was a clear keep, and it produced a land drop on each of the first five turns. **Mana:** It missed one land drop in the first six turns, in round 6, when it apparently had no land in hand, so that was not a mistake. It ended two turns with a castable card and the mana for it. Round 6: Ancestral Vision was left unsuspended with 2 sources open, which is defensible because Terminate ({B}{R}) was being held. Round 7 was a real mistake. It finished with 4 sources plus Treasures open and Talisman of Indulgence castable, then discarded Island, Luxury Suite, Aqueous Form and Replicating Ring to get down to seven in cleanup. Talisman and Replicating Ring could have been cast with the spare mana instead of throwing them away. **Attacks:** It never skipped an attack when it had attackers. In round 6, Creeping Bloodsucker (1/2) stayed home while Wilhelt had no untapped blocker. That cost 1 damage and kept a blocker back, so it was not a meaningful mistake. In rounds 7 and 8 it held back Roaming Throne on purpose to protect the doubled Obeka trigger, and the other creatures that stayed home were summoning sick. The rest of its attacks were well aimed: Obeka went at players with no untapped blockers (Zuko in round 5, Wilhelt in rounds 6 and 7), and she connected every time.

## Dropped by the fact check

- This list has no maximum hand size unless Reliquary Tower is out. Record: Reliquary Tower's text is 'You have no maximum hand size.' Without it the deck has the normal maximum of seven, which is why the pilot discarded down to seven in round 7 (turn 25) cleanup. The claim has the rule backwards.
- An Obeka hit for N therefore means N more cards from an opponent's library cast for free. Record: Court of Locthwain exiles the top card of the opponent's library, which can be a land. In round 7 (turn 25) the four extra upkeeps exiled Drakuseth, Scaled Nurturer, Dracogenesis and a Mountain. The Mountain could not be cast. Only Dracogenesis was cast with a Court monarch permission; Drakuseth and Scaled Nurturer were cast free through Dracogenesis. An Obeka hit for N does not guarantee N free casts.
