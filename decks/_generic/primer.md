# Playing Commander well at this table

These hold for any deck. Your own deck's primer comes after this and knows its cards better.

## Opening hand
- Keep a seven with three or four lands, or two with cheap mana beside them, that makes the colours of your first plays and your commander, and that has something to do in the first three turns. The first mulligan is free: use it on any hand that falls short. Hands kept on two lands and no ramp, one land, or no source of the commander's colours have lost games here.

## Your turn
- Spend your mana every turn, unless you are holding it for a particular instant you expect to use before your next turn.
- Cast creatures with haste, and anything that changes combat, before combat, not after.
- When your commander dies or is exiled, the table returns it to the command zone. Recast it: the tax is almost always worth paying, and a deck without its commander is half a deck.

## Attacking
- Attack when the damage matters and the defender has no good block. Before sending everything, count what each opponent can attack you with on their own turns (they all untap first) and what you would have left to block. One pilot here attacked with everything two turns running and went from 40 life to out in two rounds.
- Attack the player who is winning, not the player who hit you last.

## Holding and using interaction
- Removal, counters and protection are for what decides the game: the player closest to winning, an engine or combo turn, an evasive threat you cannot block, or removal aimed at your commander.
- The time to use an answer is usually during the big turn, not after it. A player who casts many spells in a turn, takes extra upkeeps, combats or turns, or steals other players' cards is the real threat now. One pilot held Chaos Warp through a 122-action turn, "saving it for a real threat", while that turn stole two of the Dragons that killed it three turns later.
- Don't tap out on your own turn while you hold the only answer to a threat already on the board. One pilot tapped every white source with Path to Exile in hand, against a flier it could not block, which went on to deal it 26 commander damage.

## Reading the table
- Before each of your turns, ask who is winning: life, board, cards in hand, commander damage, and what each deck is trying to do (the other players have said what their decks do). Keep the leader in check, and don't become the leader early if you cannot defend it.
- Say so at the table when someone is about to win. Deals are not binding, but a table that agrees on the threat tends to act on it.
