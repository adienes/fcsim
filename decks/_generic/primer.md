# Playing Commander well at this table

These hold for any deck. Your own deck's primer comes after and knows its cards better.

## Opening hand
- Keep a seven with three or four lands — or two plus cheap ramp — that casts your early plays and your commander's colours, and that does something in the first three turns.
- The first mulligan is free; spend it. Hands kept on two lands with no ramp, one land, or no source of the commander's colours have lost games here.

## Your turn
- Spend all your mana every turn unless you are holding a specific instant you expect to cast before your next turn.
- Haste creatures and anything that changes combat go down precombat.
- Recast your commander when it dies or is exiled. The tax is almost always worth paying.

## Attacking
- Attack when the damage matters and the defender has no good block. First count what each opponent can swing at you on their untapped turn and what you would have left to block. One pilot here attacked with everything two turns running and went 40 to dead in two rounds.
- Attack whoever is winning, not whoever hit you last.
- *(Seen once.)* If an opponent is in single digits and the others hit him every turn, let them finish him. Game 0062: this seat tapped both creatures for the last 12 on a player already taking 5, 5 and 11; two turns later Miirym aimed two 7-damage triggers and four attackers here instead of at the other live seat, and this seat died while the game ran six more turns. The kill buys nothing the table was about to give you, frees the leader's attacks, and marks you as the seat that removes players.

## Holding and using interaction
- Answers are for what decides the game: the player closest to winning, an engine or combo turn, unblockable evasion, removal aimed at your commander.
- Use the answer *during* the big turn, not after. One pilot held Chaos Warp through a 122-action turn "saving it for a real threat" — that turn stole two of the Dragons that killed it three turns later.
- Don't tap out while you hold the only answer to a threat already on board. One pilot tapped every white source with Path to Exile in hand against a flier it could not block, and took 26 commander damage.
- A spell on the stack aimed at your permanent is a deadline, not a stop sign: cash in its tap abilities and every cheap sacrifice its triggers convert into damage or cards first. Game 0061: with a wrath on the stack, the pilot fetched and cast Roiling Regrowth for two pings and two drains, killing one opponent and putting two others to 5.

## Pricing your own life
- When a repeating tax offers "discard or lose life", pay in cards whenever your hand holds surplus lands or spells you will never cast. Game 0061: Obeka's Court of Ambition twice offered "discard two or lose 6"; the pilot paid life both times, 40 → 24, while sitting on a spare Aqueduct, a Swamp and an uncastable sorcery — then discarded to hand size anyway on turn 27. It died on turn 28 without ever being attacked for lethal; those 12 life were the whole margin.
- Total the incidental drains already on board before you sell life. Fetches, pain lands and self-damaging removal are real costs when every player has a one- or two-point outlet.

## Reading the table
- Each turn, name the leader: life, board, hand, commander damage, stated plan. Check the leader; don't become the leader early if you cannot defend the crown.
- A spectacular turn buys the table's full attention. Don't end it tapped out with no blocker and no instant — price defence into the turn. In Game 0061 Obeka drained three opponents for 12 and killed a commander, then passed with two mana and nothing to hold while two opponents announced they were coming for the crown; 27 → 4 in a round and a half, first player out. Take announcements at face value.
- Say it aloud, to the table, on the turn it matters. Game 0062: this seat held the highest life and a 9/9 flier, planned to ask another player to attack Miirym, and never addressed the table once — while that player did talk to Miirym and spent removal accordingly. Miirym attacked this seat.
- Deals are not binding, but a table that agrees on the threat acts on it.

## After a sweeper
- *(Seen once.)* A board wipe does not solve a deck that rebuilds faster than you. Game 0062: Farewell exiled five of Miirym's creatures and both of this seat's threats; Miirym had a new fat flier the next turn and six attackers by turn 33, while this seat spent two turns and five cards to field four creatures at 8 life. Before you wipe, ask whose next three draws refill quicker, and keep something to punish the rebuild.
